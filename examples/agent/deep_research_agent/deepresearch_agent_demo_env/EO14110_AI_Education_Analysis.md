# Analysis of AI Education Provisions in Executive Order 14110
## Based on Congressional Research Service Report R47843

### Executive Summary
Executive Order 14110, "Safe, Secure, and Trustworthy Development and Use of Artificial Intelligence," signed on October 30, 2023, contains limited but significant provisions related to AI education. The primary education mandate is found in Section 8(d) under "Consumer Protection," requiring the Department of Education to develop resources, policies, and guidance for AI use in education, including an AI toolkit for educators.

### 1. Education Standards Analysis

**CRS Report Findings:**
- The CRS report does not identify specific education standards or curriculum standards for AI in EO 14110.
- The order focuses on developing "resources, policies, and guidance" rather than establishing formal educational standards.
- Any standards development would be implied within the resources and guidance to be developed by the Department of Education.

**Gaps in Standard-Setting:**
- No mention of K-12 AI literacy standards
- No reference to existing AI education frameworks (e.g., ISTE standards, AI4K12 initiative)
- No alignment with state educational standards

### 2. Curriculum Development Frameworks

**CRS Report Findings:**
- Section 8(d) requires development of "an AI toolkit for educators"
- The toolkit is presumably intended to support curriculum integration of AI concepts
- No specific curriculum frameworks are mandated or referenced in the EO

**Implementation Approach:**
- Toolkit development suggests a resource-based rather than prescriptive curriculum approach
- Likely to include sample lessons, teaching strategies, and integration guides
- No timeline or process specified for curriculum framework development

### 3. Teacher Training Requirements

**CRS Report Findings:**
- The AI toolkit is specifically targeted at "educators"
- Implicitly addresses teacher training through resource provision
- No explicit teacher professional development requirements or programs

**Additional Relevant Provisions:**
- Section 6(c) directs NSF to "prioritize available resources to support AI-related education and AI-related workforce development through existing programs"
- This could include teacher training programs through NSF's existing education initiatives

### 4. Institutional Mandates

**Primary Responsible Agency:**
- **Department of Education (ED)**: Mandated to develop AI education resources, policies, and guidance by October 29, 2024 (365 days from EO issuance)

**Supporting Agencies:**
- **National Science Foundation (NSF)**: Directed to prioritize resources for AI-related education and workforce development
- **Other agencies**: May contribute through the White House AI Council coordination

**Governance Structure:**
- White House Artificial Intelligence Council established to coordinate implementation
- Council includes heads of ED, NSF, and other relevant agencies
- No separate AI Education Task Force established in EO 14110

### 5. Implementation Mechanisms

**Timeline:**
- ED must complete development of AI education resources by October 29, 2024
- No intermediate milestones specified in EO or CRS report

**Coordination Mechanisms:**
- White House AI Council provides interagency coordination
- No specific education-focused implementation working group mentioned

**Reporting and Accountability:**
- No specific reporting requirements for education components beyond general EO implementation tracking
- Progress likely tracked through existing agency reporting mechanisms

### 6. Workforce Development Connections

**Section 6 - Worker Support:**
- Recognizes AI's potential disruptive impact on workforce
- Directs agencies to research and develop mitigation strategies
- NSF specifically tasked with prioritizing AI-related education and workforce development

**Implications for Education:**
- Connects K-12/higher education with workforce needs
- Suggests focus on both AI literacy and AI-related job skills
- No specific articulation between educational and workforce development components

### 7. Comparison with Broader EO Framework

**Position within EO Structure:**
- Education provisions located in "Consumer Protection" section (Section 8)
- Reflects framing of AI education as a consumer protection/empowerment issue
- Separate from "Worker Support" (Section 6) which addresses workforce impacts

**Integration with Other Policy Areas:**
- Connected to equity considerations (Section 7 on AI Bias and Civil Rights)
- Related to privacy protections (Section 9) relevant to educational data
- Tied to federal use of AI (Section 10) which includes government training

### 8. CRS Analysis Methodology

**Report Structure:**
- Organized by EO policy areas (8 sections)
- Uses tables to list requirements, lead agencies, due dates
- Provides minimal interpretive analysis beyond categorization

**Coverage of Education Provisions:**
- Accurately identifies Section 8(d) as main education provision
- Notes inclusion of AI toolkit requirement
- Places education in consumer protection context

**Limitations:**
- No analysis of education-specific implementation challenges
- No discussion of existing AI education initiatives
- Limited contextualization within broader education policy landscape

### 9. Key Observations

1. **Limited Scope**: EO 14110's education provisions are relatively narrow compared to comprehensive AI education strategies seen in other countries.

2. **Resource-Focused Approach**: Emphasis on developing resources rather than mandating curriculum changes or teacher certification requirements.

3. **Department-Led Implementation**: Primary responsibility rests with ED rather than a cross-sector task force.

4. **Workforce Alignment**: Education provisions connected to broader workforce development concerns.

5. **Timeline Constraints**: 365-day timeline for resource development may be ambitious given complexity of AI education.

### 10. Implications for Policy Implementation

**Strengths:**
- Clear agency assignment (ED)
- Specific deliverable (AI toolkit)
- Connection to broader AI governance framework

**Challenges:**
- Limited funding mechanisms specified
- No stakeholder engagement requirements
- Potential overlap with existing state/local education authority
- Need for coordination with NSF and other agencies' education programs

**Recommendations for Enhanced Implementation:**
1. Establish education-specific working group within White House AI Council
2. Engage educational stakeholders (teachers, administrators, edtech companies)
3. Align with existing AI education initiatives and standards
4. Develop phased implementation with pilot programs
5. Create evaluation framework for AI toolkit effectiveness

*Analysis based on CRS Report R47843, "Highlights of the 2023 Executive Order on Artificial Intelligence for Congress," November 17, 2023.*
