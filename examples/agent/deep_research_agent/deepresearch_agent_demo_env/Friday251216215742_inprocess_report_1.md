# Time Required for Eliud Kipchoge to Run from Earth to the Moon at His Record Marathon Pace

- Eliud Kipchoge’s official marathon world record, recognized by World Athletics and major sports authorities, is **2 hours, 1 minute, and 39 seconds (2:01:39)**, which he achieved at the **2018 Berlin Marathon**. This time corresponds to a marathon distance of **42.195 kilometers**. To calculate his average speed, we first convert his time into hours: 2 hours + (1 minute ÷ 60) + (39 seconds ÷ 3600) = 2 + 0.01667 + 0.01083 ≈ **2.0275 hours**. His average speed is therefore 42.195 km ÷ 2.0275 h ≈ **20.81 km/h**. Alternatively, in meters per second: 42,195 meters ÷ (2×3600 + 1×60 + 39) = 42,195 ÷ 7299 ≈ **5.781 m/s**. It is important to note that while Kipchoge ran a faster time of **1:59:40** during the INEOS 1:59 Challenge in Vienna in 2019, this performance is **not recognized as an official world record** due to the use of rotating pacemakers, a closed course, and other non-standard conditions not permitted under World Athletics rules. Therefore, for official pace calculations, the **2:01:39 Berlin Marathon time remains the valid benchmark**. Using this official pace ensures compliance with standard athletic record-keeping practices and provides a realistic, competition-legal speed for extrapolation over extreme distances. Using his speed of **20.81 km/h**, the time required is: 356,500 km ÷ 20.81 km/h ≈ **17,131.2 hours**. Rounding this to the nearest **1,000 hours** yields **17,000 hours**. For 356,500 km, total time = 356,500 × 172.97 ≈ **61,663,805 seconds**. Converting to hours: 61,663,805 ÷ 3600 ≈ **17,128.8 hours**, which again rounds to **17,000 hours** when rounded to the nearest thousand.
