### Eliud Kipchoge's Official Marathon World Record and Moon Perigee Distance Calculation

- Eliud Kipchoge’s official marathon world record is **2:01:09**, set on **25 September 2022** at the **Berlin Marathon**, as confirmed by multiple authoritative sources including the **World Athletics official records page**, which lists his time as **2:01:09** under the "Marathon – men – senior – all" category, ranking him second only to Kelvin Kiptum’s 2:00:35 (set in Chicago, 2023) [World Athletics All-Time Top Lists](https://worldathletics.org/records/all-time-toplists/road-running/marathon/outdoor/men/senior). Additional confirmation comes from the Abbott World Marathon Majors profile, which states that Kipchoge “smashed his own world record at the BMW BERLIN-MARATHON, where he ran 2:01:09” [AbbottWMM Profile](https://www.worldmarathonmajors.com/elite-series/champions-profile/eliud-kipchoge). This time supersedes his previous world record of **2:01:39**, set in Berlin in 2018. The standard marathon distance used for world record ratification is **42.195 kilometers**, as defined by the International Association of Athletics Federations (IAAF), now World Athletics. Therefore, Kipchoge’s average pace can be calculated precisely: total time in seconds is (2 × 3600) + (1 × 60) + 9 = **7269 seconds**. Dividing this by 42.195 km yields a pace of **172.27 seconds per kilometer**, or **2 minutes and 52.27 seconds per kilometer**, which equates to **0.17227 seconds per meter**. This pace represents one of the fastest sustained human running speeds ever recorded over such a long distance and forms the basis for extrapolating how long it would take to cover astronomical distances like the Earth–Moon perigee.

- The minimum perigee distance between the Earth and the Moon—the closest approach in its elliptical orbit—is listed on the **Wikipedia page for the Moon** as **356,500 kilometers**. According to the “Orbit” section of the article, “The actual distance varies over the course of the orbit of the Moon, from **356,500 km** at perigee to 406,700 km at apogee” [Wikipedia: Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon). This value of **356,500 km** is the smallest recorded perigee distance and is the figure specified in the task for use in calculations. Using Kipchoge’s pace of **172.27 seconds per kilometer**, the total time required to run this distance is: 356,500 km × 172.27 s/km = **61,404,255 seconds**. Converting this into hours: 61,404,255 ÷ 3600 ≈ **17,056.74 hours**. Rounding to the nearest **1000 hours** gives **17000 hours**. This calculation assumes perfect maintenance of pace without fatigue, rest, or environmental constraints—purely a theoretical extrapolation based on his world-record performance. The result demonstrates the immense scale of even the shortest Earth–Moon distance when compared to human athletic capabilities, requiring nearly two full years of nonstop running at the absolute peak of human endurance performance.
