# CRS Report Analysis: AI Education Provisions in Executive Order 14110

## Report Overview
- **Report Title**: Highlights of the 2023 Executive Order on Artificial Intelligence for Congress
- **Report Number**: R47843
- **Publication Date**: November 17, 2023 (Version 3)
- **Authors**: Laurie A. Harris (Analyst in Science and Technology Policy), Chris Jaikaran (Specialist in Cybersecurity Policy)
- **Source**: Congressional Research Service (CRS)
- **Purpose**: Provides a summary of requirements for agency action in EO 14110 and timelines for their deliverables

## Analytical Approach and Structure
The CRS report organizes its analysis around the eight overarching policy areas identified in EO 14110:
1. Safety and security
2. Innovation and competition
3. Worker support
4. Consideration of AI bias and civil rights
5. Consumer protection
6. Privacy
7. Federal use of AI
8. International leadership

For each policy area, the report presents tables listing requirements with explicit deliverables and due dates, along with responsible agencies. The report focuses on extracting and tabulating the concrete actions mandated by the executive order rather than providing interpretive analysis or policy recommendations.

## AI Education Provisions Analysis

### 1. Direct AI Education Mandate (Section 8: Consumer Protection)

**Table 5, Row 9 (Section 8(d)):**
- **Requirement**: "Develop resources, policies, and guidance for AI use in education."
- **Lead Agency**: Department of Education (ED)
- **Due Date**: October 29, 2024 (365 days from EO issuance)
- **Specific Mandate**: "Shall include development of an AI toolkit for educators."
- **Policy Area**: Consumer Protection

**Key Analysis Points:**
- This is the ONLY explicit, deadline-bound education-specific requirement in EO 14110
- The requirement is categorized under "Consumer Protection," framing education stakeholders (students, families) as consumers needing protection from AI-related harms
- The deliverable is an "AI toolkit for educators" - suggesting a practical, resource-oriented approach rather than prescriptive standards
- No explicit mention of curriculum standards, teacher certification requirements, or K-12 integration mandates

### 2. Related Education and Workforce Provisions

#### A. Worker Support (Section 6)
**Table 3 and Section 6(c):**
- NSF directed to "prioritize available resources to support AI-related education and AI-related workforce development through existing programs"
- No specific deliverables or deadlines for this directive
- Focus appears to be on post-secondary and workforce training rather than K-12 education

#### B. Federal Use of AI (Section 10)
**Table 7, Multiple Rows:**
- Requires federal agencies (including ED) to implement AI training programs for employees (10.2(g))
- Mandates designation of Chief Artificial Intelligence Officers in all CFO Act agencies (10.1(b)(i))
- Requires creation of Artificial Intelligence Governance Boards within agencies (10.1(b)(iii))
- These provisions apply to the Department of Education as a federal agency, potentially influencing its internal capacity to develop and implement AI education policies

### 3. Governance and Coordination Mechanisms

#### White House Artificial Intelligence Council
- Established by EO 14110 to coordinate timely implementation of federal AI policies
- **Includes Department of Education** as a member (listed among 28 departments/agencies)
- Chaired by the Assistant to the President and Deputy Chief of Staff for Policy
- Provides high-level interagency coordination but no specific education-focused subgroup

#### Interagency Council on Federal Use of AI (Section 10.1(a))
- Convened by OMB to coordinate AI use by federal agencies
- Includes all CFO Act agencies (including ED) and the Director of National Intelligence
- Focuses on federal government's internal use of AI rather than education policy

### 4. Absences and Limitations in AI Education Coverage

**Notably Missing from EO 14110 (as analyzed by CRS):**
- No specific AI education standards or curriculum frameworks
- No K-12 integration requirements or benchmarks
- No teacher training or certification mandates beyond the toolkit development
- No dedicated AI Education Task Force (unlike the 2025 executive order)
- No funding allocations specifically for AI education implementation
- No assessment or evaluation requirements for AI education outcomes

## Critical Analysis of CRS Methodology

### Analytical Strengths:
1. **Systematic Extraction**: CRS meticulously extracts every requirement with explicit deliverables and deadlines
2. **Clear Tabular Presentation**: Easy reference format for congressional staff
3. **Comprehensive Coverage**: All 8 policy areas and 100+ specific actions covered
4. **Source Transparency**: Clear citation to Federal Register publication

### Analytical Limitations:
1. **Descriptive而非 Prescriptive**: Reports what the EO says rather than analyzing implementation challenges or policy implications
2. **No Gap Analysis**: Does not identify missing elements or potential shortcomings in the EO's approach
3. **Limited Context**: Minimal discussion of how these provisions relate to existing education policies or programs
4. **No Stakeholder Perspective**: Does not incorporate views from education organizations, states, or districts

### Potential Biases:
1. **Congressional Service Orientation**: Focuses on information useful for oversight rather than policy evaluation
2. **Neutral/Descriptive Bias**: Avoids normative judgments which could be seen as limiting critical analysis
3. **Federal Focus**: Centers federal agency actions without considering state/local implementation realities

## Comparison with Original EO Text

The CRS analysis accurately reflects the limited education provisions in EO 14110. The original text of Section 8(d) states:

"The Secretary of Education shall, within 365 days of the date of this order, develop resources, policies, and guidance for the use of AI in education, which shall include the development of an AI toolkit for educators to support the implementation of the guidance described in this subsection."

CRS faithfully reproduces this requirement without additional interpretation or expansion.

## Implications for Education Governance

### Institutional Mandates:
1. **Department of Education**: Primary responsibility for AI education policy development
2. **Internal Capacity Building**: Must establish CAIO position and AI Governance Board
3. **Resource Development Focus**: Toolkit approach suggests voluntary guidance rather than mandatory standards

### Implementation Timeline:
- **October 29, 2024**: Deadline for ED to release AI toolkit and guidance
- **Ongoing**: NSF to prioritize AI education/resources through existing programs
- **Continuous**: Federal employee AI training requirements apply to ED staff

### Governance Model:
- **Coordinated Federal Approach**: ED works within White House AI Council framework
- **Consumer Protection Framework**: Education policy framed as consumer protection
- **Resource-Based Intervention**: Toolkit model suggests capacity-building over regulation

## Conclusion

The CRS analysis reveals that EO 14110 takes a limited, resource-focused approach to AI education governance, with only one explicit education requirement centered on toolkit development. The governance framework relies on existing departmental structures supplemented by interagency coordination, rather than creating specialized education task forces or comprehensive standards. This approach reflects the EO's broader emphasis on federal leadership and risk management rather than transformative education policy.
